Artificial Intelligence is a set of technologies that enables computers to learn, reason, and perform a variety of advanced tasks in ways that previously required human intelligence, such as understanding language, analyzing data, and even providing helpful suggestions.

Artificial Intelligence techniques, though diverse, are all fundamentally based on data, algorithms, and computing power. AI systems learn and improve through exposure to large amounts of data, identifying patterns and relationships that humans might otherwise miss. This data serves as training materials, the quality and quantity of which are critical to AI performance.

AI is not a single technology, but a broad field that encompasses several key areas:

- Machine Learning (ML): One of the most popular subfields of AI, which uses a statistical and data-driven approach. ML systems learn from data to identify patterns and make predictions or decisions without direct programming (Google). Given that ML is trained and not programmed, it is inherently uncertain and hence possesses a confidence rate in its results.
- Deep Learning (DL): DL is a subfield of machine learning which uses multi-layered artificial neural networks to learn from data. These networks are inspired by the structure of the human brain and are particularly suited to complex tasks like image and speech recognition.
- Natural Language Processing (NLP): NLP allows computers to understand, interpret and generate human language. The advent of transformers and LLMs has revolutionized NLP, allowing models to achieve complex semantic understanding (context, sarcasm, idioms, …).
- Computer Vision: It allows computers to “see” and interpret visual information, such as images and videos. It has a wide range of applications, going from facial recognition to self-driving cars
