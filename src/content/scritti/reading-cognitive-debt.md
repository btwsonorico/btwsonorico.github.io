## What it is

An MIT Media Lab study. 54 people write essays in three groups: with an LLM, with a search engine, with no tools. EEG measures their brain connectivity during the task; teachers, an AI judge and NLP analysis score the essays. In a fourth session, 18 of them switch condition.

## What I take from it

**Recall.** Minutes after finishing, about 83% of the people in the LLM group could not correctly quote a sentence from the essay they had just written. In the other two groups, about 11%. The text was produced, but not learned.

**Mental engagement.** Brain connectivity was strongest with no tools, medium with a search engine, weakest with the LLM. The LLM group also felt the least ownership of its essays.

**Evidence of a cognitive debt.** These results are evidence of an impact of AI that inevitably comes with a cognitive debt: what we save in effort now, we pay later in memory, understanding and ownership. People who moved from the LLM to no tools in the last session kept struggling to quote their work.

## Where it is weak

- **Small and narrow.** 54 people, 18 in the last session, mostly university students, 20-minute essays. It is a preprint, not yet peer-reviewed.
- **It depends on how AI is used.** People who first wrote alone and then used the LLM kept high engagement. The debt is strongest when the AI does the first draft.
- **The press went further than the paper.** The authors rejected headlines like "ChatGPT makes you dumb".
